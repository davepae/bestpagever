0 m · The surface

Below two hundred metres, sunlight gives up. What remains is the light the ocean makes for itself.

Begin the descent

0 – 200 m · Sunlight zone

A thin, bright skin on a very deep body.

Almost everything you picture when you think of the sea — reefs, kelp, sunlight dappling through water — lives in the top two hundred metres. It is where algae can still photosynthesise. Below it, the ocean keeps going.

200 – 1,000 m · Twilight zone

The largest migration on Earth happens every night.

At dusk, billions of fish, squid and krill rise from the twilight zone to feed near the surface. Before dawn they sink back into the blue. Blue is the last colour left down here, and soon there is almost none.

1,000 – 4,000 m · Midnight zone

Roughly three in four animals here make their own light.

To lure, to warn, to hide their silhouette, to find each other. With no sun to see by, the sea became its own night sky.
